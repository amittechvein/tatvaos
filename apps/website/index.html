The products

Six products, one account.

Every screen below is the real thing, photographed from a running build. Nothing here is a mock-up.

Mail

Business email on your own domain, with the things people actually miss when they leave Gmail — and the things Gmail never gave them.

  • Conversations, categories with colours, filter rules applied at delivery
  • Shared mailboxes: grants, send-as, and a switcher in the rail
  • Search across every folder and every body, with operators
  • Signatures, drafts, out-of-office replies
  • Attachments scanned for viruses; oversize ones park in Space and send as a link
  • IMAP and SMTP with app passwords, so Thunderbird and Outlook still work
Mail — inbox and reading pane

Connect

Meetings that belong to the organisation, not to whoever started them. Scheduled from the console or from the phone.

  • Live meetings first in the list, so you join the one that is happening
  • Live captions, and recordings kept for 30 days
  • Minutes by TatvaOS AI — summary, decisions, action items — for organisations that switch it on
  • Waiting room, host controls, mute all, meeting locks
  • Guests join without an account, from a link or a code
  • A meetings API, so an ERP or timetable system can create and list them
Connect — meetings

Calendar

Day, week, month and agenda. Recurrence and reminders. Invitations that arrive properly in Gmail and in Outlook — both directions.

  • Invitations carried in the exact form each of Gmail and Outlook needs, and their replies read back
  • A meeting link on the event, carried into the invitation
  • Reminders that go out by mail
Calendar — week

Space

Files for a person, for a team and for the whole organisation, out of the same allowance as their mail.

  • My Space, Organisation, Shared with me, Trash
  • Drag-and-drop upload with progress and cancel
  • Public links with an audience you choose
  • Save a mail attachment into Space without the bytes leaving the server
Space — my files

Core — the console

Where an administrator runs the organisation. People, domains, storage, keys and the record of who did what.

  • Add one person, or upload a spreadsheet of hundreds
  • Departments, roles, and one-action offboarding with mail forwarding
  • Prove you own your domain — four methods, pick the one you can actually do
  • Buy storage once, split it across Mail and Space yourself
  • An audit trail of every administrative action, filterable by person and product
Core — organisation dashboard

Your account

One place a person manages their own identity: photo, recovery details, two-step verification, signed-in devices and what their storage is being spent on.

  • Two-step verification with an authenticator app and recovery codes
  • Every signed-in device listed, and one click to sign out everywhere else
  • An alert by mail when a new device signs in
  • Storage broken down by product
  • Family contacts, kept apart from the organisation directory
Account

The platform underneath

The parts you only notice when they are missing.

A suite is only worth more than its parts if the parts share an identity, an allowance and a record. These are the pieces that make that true.

Live

One identity, every product

A person exists once in Core. Mail, Calendar, Connect and Space read that one record. Remove them once and they are gone from all of it.

Live

Isolated by the database

Organisations are separated by PostgreSQL row-level security, not by application code remembering to filter. It holds even when the code is wrong.

Live

Sign in to your other software

TatvaOS is an OpenID Connect provider. Your library system, your ERP or your intranet can use a TatvaOS account to sign people in.

Live

Two-step verification

Time-based codes from any authenticator app, recovery codes, a replay guard, and a challenge at sign-in that a stolen password cannot skip.

Live

Storage bought once, split by you

Buy one number for the organisation. Your administrator divides it between Mail and Space and rebalances whenever they like — no new purchase, no ticket.

Live

On the phone too

The TatvaOS One Android app is on Google Play. Mail and meetings on the phone, signed in with the same account — and it hands over to the browser for everything else.

Live

Hosted in India

Your mail, files, calendar and recordings live on our server in Mumbai, with a full audit trail of every administrative action taken on your organisation.

Build on it

Three APIs, each behind an organisation key your administrator issues and can revoke. A key can never create an administrator.

  • Mail API — POST /v1/mail/send, restricted to sender addresses you name
  • People API — so your own software can admit and remove people
  • Meetings API — a paged timetable an ERP can read and write
  • SSO applications — register a client, choose the claims, see the consent screen your people will see
  • A downloadable integration guide on the page itself

Week 1
4 – 9 August
99 commits
4 August

Day one: a server, a schema and a way in

Server provisioned, DNS preflight, a deliverability harness for mail. The Core schema, cloud environments and CI/CD. Sign-in with refresh-token rotation, lockout and password change. Postfix's open relay on port 25 was closed before a single message was ever sent.

5 August

The front door opens

Self-service signup with four different ways to prove you own your domain, so an administrator can pick whichever one they can actually do. Live domain verification, email and mobile OTP.

6 August

Mail becomes a product

A real mail client: list rows with chips, bulk select, a docked composer, search at the top. Sign-in by mobile OTP. Host discipline — mail only answers on its own subdomain, the console only on its own.

7 – 8 August

The composer, and the things nobody demos

Rich text, attachments, emoji, full-screen and print. Plans managed from the operator console. Profile photos. Forgot-password by email link or phone OTP — which revokes every existing session when the password changes.

Week 2
10 – 16 August
159 commits
10 – 12 August

Mail stops being a demo

Blocked senders and filter rules applied at delivery, not on display. Real search across every folder and every message body. An alert when a new device signs in. Organisation storage, with a page that shows where it went. The audit trail.

14 August

Two-step verification

TOTP with encrypted secrets, recovery codes and a replay guard; the challenge at sign-in; the enrolment QR rendered in the browser. The same day: conversation view, search operators, and one-action offboarding that forwards a leaver's mail to their successor.

15 August

Shared mailboxes, and one allowance per person

Grants, send-as and a switcher in the rail. Attachments scanned and the infected ones refused. Storage stopped being a mailbox quota and became one allowance a person spends across every product.

16 August

Calendar and Space arrive

Calendar with events, recurrence, reminders and four views. Space with a Drive-style upload tray, progress, cancel and drag-and-drop, and a landing page for public links.

Week 3
17 – 23 August
196 commits
19 – 20 August

Mail meets the rest of the world

Oversize attachments park in Space and send as a link. Calendar invitations carried in the exact form Gmail needs and the exact form Outlook needs, both directions. Deploys began proving the new API starts before retiring the old one.

21 August

TatvaOS AI gets its one door

One place — and only one — through which TatvaOS AI runs, and it refuses any call that is not scoped to an organisation that has switched it on. Meeting minutes go through it.

23 August

Recordings made to pay for themselves

Recordings count against the owner's own quota and age off after 30 days. The personal pricing was costed against measured numbers rather than guesses: AI minutes at about ₹1 per meeting-hour, captions at nothing.

Week 4
24 – 30 August
93 commits
26 – 27 August

Filing

Categories. Saving a received attachment straight into Space without the bytes leaving the server. Folders you can rename and delete without deleting the mail inside them. Keyboard shortcuts, and a list of them you can actually find.

29 – 31 August

Categories become visible

A swatch picker, named colour chips in the list, bulk colouring. The reading-pane toolbar redrawn — one labelled Reply as the row's only outlined control, and the destructive actions moved behind their own divider.

Week 5
31 Aug – 6 September
147 commits
3 September

TatvaOS becomes something you can build on

API keys and a send endpoint, so a website form or a billing job can post mail through the platform. A keys page with the how-to on it, a downloadable integration guide, and keys restricted to the sender addresses you name.

5 September

The phone signs in

The Android app got a real API client, two-factor, session restore, and a dashboard showing only the tiles that person is entitled to.

Week 6
7 – 13 September
145 commits
7 September

One kit, every page

Shared controls across signup, the seven organisation pages, the account page, the admin area and Family — so a field looks and behaves the same wherever you meet it.

9 – 11 September

People, at volume

Hire and People entered the catalogue. Bulk create arrived: download a template, fill it in, upload it — including a guard for the phone numbers a spreadsheet has quietly rounded into scientific notation.

13 September

Design before code

Two decisions written down and reviewed before anything was built: what a person is entitled to is derived from their organisation's plan rather than copied into rows that drift; and how signing in on a phone hands over to the browser.

Week 7
14 – 20 September
208 commits — the heaviest week
15 September

The database takes its rights back

UPDATE and DELETE were revoked from the application on five record tables — the audit trail among them — so a bug cannot rewrite history. CI asserts both directions: that the allowed writes still work, and that the forbidden ones still fail.

16 September

The rewrite lands in production

The purchased template was deleted and the shell rebuilt on one stylesheet of our own. The same deploy carried the mobile-to-browser sign-in handoff, and a fix so that changing your recovery details now requires your current password.

17 September

Single sign-on goes live

TatvaOS became an OpenID Connect provider, deployed in stages across one day, with the production signing key rotated the same morning and rate limits put in front of it. The first real sign-on against production passed that evening.

18 September

Consent, and keys for your own software

The consent screen redesigned around what a person is actually agreeing to. Organisation API keys for Mail, People and SSO — with the hard rule that a key can never create an administrator.

19 September

A meetings API, and a live incident

A paged timetable API so an ERP can read and create meetings. That same day a live meeting hit a guest ceiling nobody knew was there — 200 guests, refused silently. It was found, raised to 2,000, and given a warning log, the same day.

Week 8
21 – 23 September
18 commits, so far
21 September

Invitations that land you signed in

A welcome note and product tiles in the invitation mail, and one-use sign-in links a manager can send from the People page. Accepting an invitation now ends any old sessions and signs you straight in — through the two-factor challenge if you have one.

22 September

TatvaOS One reaches Google Play

The Android app published — mail and meetings on the phone, signing in with the same account. Taking card payment is the next thing to build.
